Beef

Although beef is a high quality, complete protein, it has one of the highest environmental footprints of any common food source. Cattle produce methane through digestion and require ample land for grazing and feed production. Additionally, waste management also contributes to the environmental impact of cattle.

Tofu

Tofu (made of soybeans) is a high quality plant-based protein and contains all 9 essential amino acids as well as iron and fiber. Soy production requires land use, but not as much as is needed for feed production and animal grazing. This keeps the environmental impact relatively low.

    Simulation method

    Emissions are estimated as (grams of protein per day ÷ 100) × category emissions factor (kg CO₂e per 100 g protein). Baseline values are illustrative assumptions, not measured consumption. The original prototype supplies factors for beef (49.89), chicken (5.70), eggs (4.21) and pulses (0.84). For dairy, pork, tofu and nuts, this expanded demonstration uses approximate category factors (10.82, 7.61, 1.98 and 0.26 respectively), which should be verified against the final selected dataset before publication. Dairy is represented by a cheese-category proxy, legumes by other pulses, and nuts by a simplified proxy. Values are not personalized forecasts.

    Background: Our World in Data — Environmental impacts of food and Poore & Nemecek (2018) .
