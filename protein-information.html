Beef

Although beef is a high quality, complete protein, it has one of the highest environmental footprints of any common food source. Cattle produce methane through digestion and require ample land for grazing and feed production. Additionally, waste management also contributes to the environmental impact of cattle.

Dairy

Dairy products provide complete protein and calcium with good digestibility. However, due to dairy product's origin (cows), dairy production contributes greatly to greenhouse gas emissions in the same ways as beef.

Pork

Pork is a complete protein, but feed production, manure management, and land and water use for pig raising contribute greatly to pork's environmental impact.

Chicken

Alongside being a complete protein, chicken also provides B vitamins and selenium. Chicken production tends to have a lower environmental impact than beef and pork, but still requires feed, land, and waste management.

Would you make this change?

Eggs

Eggs are a complete protein with B vitamins, choline, and selenium. Eggs are a byproduct of chickens so the environmental impact is largely similar to that of chicken.

Would you make this change?

Tofu

Tofu (made of soybeans) is a high quality plant-based protein and contains all 9 essential amino acids as well as iron and fiber. Soy production requires land use, but not as much as is needed for feed production and animal grazing. This keeps the environmental impact relatively low.

Would you make this change?

Legumes

Other legumes may be relatively low in some essential amino acids, but still provide fiber, complex carbohydrates, iron, and other essential nutrients. These protein sources tend to have very low greenhouse gas emissions compared to animal protein sources. Main considerations are land use and water use.

Would you make this change?

Nuts

Nuts are a protein source as well as a source of fiber, vitamin E, and magnesium. The quality of protein varies by nut type. Nuts tend to have far lower greenhouse gas emssions, but some varieties require substatial amounts of irrigation water.
