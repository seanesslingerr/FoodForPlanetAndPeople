What is protein quality?

Your body needs 9 essential amino acids that it can't make on its own, so they have to come from food. Not every protein supplies all 9 in the amounts you need, and you don't digest every protein equally well.

Protein quality is scored with DIAAS, a standard from the UN's Food and Agriculture Organization that accounts for both. A score of 100 or more means the protein covers every essential amino acid. Peas score 73, meaning that per gram of protein, they supply only about 73% of the target amount of the amino acid they're lowest in.

What counts for the planet?

The greenhouse gas emissions from producing 100 grams of protein, counting everything from land use and farming through processing, transport, and retail. It's measured in kilograms of CO2 equivalent, which puts gases like methane on the same scale as carbon dioxide.

      How the score works

      Q is protein quality: the food's DIAAS score divided by 100. DIAAS measures how well a protein supplies the 9 essential amino acids your body can't make, after accounting for how much you digest. Anything at 100 or above already covers every essential amino acid, so it's capped at 1; a higher score wouldn't do your body any more good. E is a climate score from greenhouse gas emissions per 100 g of protein, on a log scale: the lowest emitter scores 1 and the highest scores 0. The two are multiplied, so a protein can't make up for a terrible score on one side with a great score on the other.

      Emissions: Poore and Nemecek (2018) via Our World in Data. Protein quality: published DIAAS values, see DIAAS overview and this review. Cheese and pork DIAAS values are typical estimates for their category.
